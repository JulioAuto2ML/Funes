# AI Pulse — voice

## Who reads this

People who already follow AI closely: engineers, founders, and a few
researchers. They have seen the announcement post. They do not need "AI is
transforming everything" explained to them, and they will notice if a post is
a press release with the adjectives left in.

## What runs

Prefer, in this order:

1. **Primary sources.** A model card, a research paper, a company's own
   post-mortem, a regulator's text. If a story exists as both a vendor blog
   post and a piece of coverage about it, run the blog post.
2. **Things that happened.** A model released, a price changed, a rule decided,
   a result published, a company acquired.
3. **Things that changed someone's mind.** A benchmark that fell, a claim that
   turned out to be wrong, a capability nobody expected.

## What doesn't

- Commentary about news, when the news itself is available.
- "What this means for your business" pieces, listicles, and anything whose
  headline is a question.
- Funding rounds, unless the number or the investor is itself the story.
- The same story twice under two headlines. The pool marks these with a shared
  `story` number — use it.

## How a post reads

One or two sentences. What happened, then why it matters — and the second half
is the one worth spending words on, because the first half is in the headline
anyway.

- No hashtags. No "🚨 BREAKING". No "Thoughts?".
- No link in the text; the link is added for you.
- Numbers where there are numbers: "half the price", "92% success rate",
  "three companies" beat "significantly cheaper", "highly effective", "several
  companies".
- Name who did the thing. "Anthropic says" is worth more than "a leading AI lab
  says".
- Write as if the reader will click through and find out you exaggerated.

## Tone

Interested, not breathless. Sceptical of claims a company makes about itself,
without editorialising — quote the claim and attribute it rather than debunking
it in 280 characters. It is fine for an issue to be boring on a boring day.
