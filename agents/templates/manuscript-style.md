# Manuscript style rules

Copy this file to the root of the manuscript workspace as `style.md` and edit
it: it is the one thing `book-editor` reads before it edits anything, and
whatever it says is what the agent enforces. The rules below are one working
example — a technical non-fiction manuscript on Chicago 16th — not a standard
Funes imposes. Delete every line that isn't yours.

The agent carries no style rules of its own. That is deliberate: a second
manuscript is a second workspace with a different `style.md`, not a second
agent and not a prompt edit.

## Reference

- Chicago Manual of Style, 16th edition.
- Author-date citations: (Author Year) in text, full References section at the
  end. No footnotes for citations; footnotes are for substantive asides only.

## Punctuation

- Do not use em-dashes. Use commas, colons, semicolons, or restructure the
  sentence.
- Do not use en-dashes in running text; spell out with "to" or "through".
  En-dashes are only for ranges in tables and figures.
- Use the serial comma (red, white, and blue).
- No periods in abbreviations except U.S. (so: PhD, BA, UK, UN).

## Spelling and word forms

- Spell out numbers under 10; use numerals for 10 and above.
- Do not hyphenate prefixes (non, pre, co, re, over, post, semi, sub, super,
  multi) unless the word looks awkward or might be misread.
- Do not double the l: canceled, traveled, totaled.
- Capitalize the second word in hyphenated compound modifiers in titles.

## Structure

- Word target per chapter: 3,000–3,500 words. Total manuscript:
  60,000–70,000 words.
- Figure callouts in text: [Figure X.Y] with the caption below. No embedded
  images in the manuscript file.
- The chapter plan lives in `chapter_outlines.md`; a chapter is checked
  against its entry there, not against a general idea of the book.
